# Cell Biology





## Cell Biology

### Diffusion

Diffusion is the spreading out of particles in a solution or a gas, resulting in a movement from an area of high
concentration to an area of low concentration down the concentration gradient.

Oxygen and carbon dioxide are transported in and out of cells by diffusion in gas exchange, and urea is transported from
cells into the blood plasma for excretion.

#### Factors affecting the rate of diffusion:

- Concentration gradient
- Temperature
- Surface area of exchange surface

Single-celled organisms have a large surface area to volume ratio, so rate of diffusion is enough to transfer the molecules
the organism needs.

Multi-cellular organisms required specialised exchange surfaces and organ systems to ensure enough molecules are transferred
for the organism.

#### Factors for good exchange surface effectiveness

- Large surface area
- Thin membrane for short diffusion path
- Efficient blood supply
- Ventilation for gaseous exchange

### Osmosis

Osmosis is the diffusion of water from an area of high water potential to an area of low water potential through a
semi-permeable membrane down the concentration gradient.

### Active Transport

Active transport requires energy, moving substances against a concentration gradient. It allows mineral ions to be absorbed
into plant root hair cells, even when the soil has a very low concentration of nutrients. It also allows sugar molecules
to be absorbed into the blood from the gut, which has a relatively low concentration of it.
